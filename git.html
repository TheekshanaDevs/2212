Traditional ML vs Transformer Foundations

Machine Learning historically worked as a "Fruit Recognizer"—classifying objects from labeled pictures. The deep learning boom allowed recognizing millions of objects, but only the 2017 Transformer breakthrough gave computers the contextual library of human language.

Conceptual Intuitions & Analogies

Understand Difficult Concepts

Zero academic jargon. The trickiest university concepts explained with clear mental models, visual diagrams, and real-life comparisons.

Lectures 01 & 02

Recognizers vs The Library Reader

The Mental Model: Teaching a child to identify apples vs giving a child every book ever printed in human history.

1. Traditional ML (The Recognizer): You show a child 50 apples and 50 oranges. They learn shape and color. Limitation: It cannot write a poem about an apple or invent apple pie recipes.
2. Deep Learning (Super Recognizer): You show millions of photos. It distinguishes a Honeycrisp apple from a Gala apple. Limitation: Still cannot reason or speak sentences.
3. The Transformer (Context Maestro): The child reads all human writing. It understands culinary history, mythology, symbolism, and relationships. It generates brand-new stories!
Lecture 04

Zero-Shot vs One-Shot vs Few-Shot

The Mental Model: Taking an exam blindfolded vs taking an exam after reviewing 3 worked answer keys.

Zero-Shot: You give zero examples. "Summarize this article." Fast, but higher probability of generic tone or unwanted length.
One-Shot: You provide 1 sample input/output pair. "Like this: [Sample text → 2-bullet summary]. Now do this new article."
Few-Shot: You provide 3-5 patterned examples. Teaches nuance, exact phrasing, and strict JSON or bullet formats.
Lecture 05

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting

The Mental Model: Showing your math scratchwork instead of guessing the final number in 1 second.

Because LLMs generate text token by token based on probabilities, asking for a direct final answer forces the model to predict the outcome prematurely.

Lecture Slide Example:

"Explain how a bill becomes law in Sri Lanka in simple numbered steps."

  1. Idea proposed by Ministry or Committee
  2. Parliament debates, amends, and takes votes
  3. Speaker signs and legal Gazette certification
Lecture 07

Text-to-Image Diffusion: Noise to Clarity

The Mental Model: A sculptor looking at static TV snow and slowly carving out pixels that look like your description.

Diffusion models start with pure random static noise. Step-by-step, the model subtracts noise that doesn't fit the text prompt until a crystal-clear image emerges.

1. Pure Static Noise
2. Reverse Denoise
3. Polished Art
Lecture 12

From Chatbots to Autonomous "Doers"

The Mental Model: A telephone advisor who gives advice vs an executive assistant who opens your calendar, sends emails, and books flights.

Chatbot (Passive)

Waits for questions, spits out text, cannot touch software or take external actions.

AI Agent (Active Doer)

Observes goals, accesses tools/APIs, loops through subtasks until an objective is finished.

Lecture 11 (Ethics)

Algorithmic Bias & The Feedback Loop

The Mental Model: If a mirror only ever looks at one neighborhood, it believes the whole world lives in that one house.

AI does not have malice; it has statistical reflection. When training datasets over-represent urban resumes or historical hiring trends favor one gender, the AI codifies this bias as the "optimal" mathematical standard.

Lecture Case Study: Scholarship system favoring urban schools due to historical internet data density.
